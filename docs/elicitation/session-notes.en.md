# Elicitation session notes

[Português](session-notes.pt.md)

Raw notes organised by question. Reminder: **People, Documents, Processes**.

## Question 1: What should the software address?

- Communication between producers and consumers.
- Facilitate payment processes.
- Consumers are called **co-producers**, because they are also engaged in the production process.
- A co-producer can support farmers (investment) and commit to buying all or part of the production.
- The system must support multiple producers and multiple co-producers.

## Question 2: User types and how it works

- There are several types of AMAP, with distinct rules.
- Farmers already know what they must produce for the next year (for example); this is agreed with the co-producers (to be confirmed).
- Consumers know all producers by name.
- The farmer creates the offers (wine, vegetables, food) and quantities. The co-producer states the product wanted, the quantity and the period of time.
- Baskets can be requested in advance.
- Concepts: Offer and Delivery.

## Question 3: Monetisation and the AMAP spirit

The AMAP movement rejects pure commodification and predatory commissions on food. How should the service be monetised?

- Several models exist.
- Annual CSA fee for support: the answer does not believe in this solution. Preference for a minimum fee per order.
- Free models exist; an annual rate also exists.
- Best model according to the answer: based on transactions (minimum fee) and the volume of co-producers.
- The system should support small producers, possibly starting free or with a very small rate to kick off.

## Question 4: Cycles

- Seasonal cycles.
- Farmers drop off produce at a specific location and co-producers pick it up.
- Co-producers must be notified of the location.

## Question 5: Discovering and joining a CSA

- The international CSA web page suggests the nearest CSA.
- On contact, if there are no vacancies, the user is placed on a waiting list.
- The user looks for the nearest CSA or the one of interest and joins, checking for vacancies or joining a waiting list.
- The co-producer subscribes to offers.

## Question 6: Primary target audience

In the first phase, is the focus on attracting farmers to start an AMAP, formalising existing informal groups, or attracting the general public to the CSA model?

- The focus is to streamline the process and be able to monetise through a **CSA portal**.
- Energise the CSA market.
- The focus is **not** to create CSAs.
- Portugal has only a few dozen CSAs.
- International solution.
- Goal: facilitate.
- User profile that filters what they like.
- Product baskets.
- Two types of consumers:
  - Organic, motivated by the food itself.
  - Ideological, motivated by working directly with farmers.

## Question 7: Failures (by producers or consumers)

- CSA management is an important actor.
- There are cycles of abundance and scarcity. Those who commit already know deliveries will be smaller or larger. The software must account for quantities across the year, and by the end there must be balance.
- The software allows posting news and communications, with very close proximity between producers and consumers. These adjustments are therefore normal and both sides understand them.

## Question 9: Quantifying sustainability

How should the software reflect the commitment to the SDGs? Should the platform calculate and show indicators (food miles saved, CO₂ avoided, food waste avoided, fair pay per hour of farm work)?

- Aims: reduce poverty, build a more sustainable society, protect natural resources.
- Farmers can/should upload certificates (or, for smaller cases, photos and descriptions).
- Some KPIs could be interesting (nice to have, not essential).

## Question 10: Who issues the invoice?

- It depends on the legal setup of the CSA. Sometimes the CSA is fully registered; other times it is very small and payment goes directly to the farmer, who issues the invoice.
- Optimise the payment system, avoiding hundreds of individual payments (investigate existing solutions).
